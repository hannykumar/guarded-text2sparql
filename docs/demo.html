02

Why an AI can't do this alone

Every thing in the graph has a unique ID, and the IDs are not guessable. Ask "which department is Ms. Brant in?" and the AI has to name her exactly. It can't. So code looks the name up first and hands over the real ID.

What the AI invents

prodi:karen-brant

Plausible. Doesn't exist. The query returns nothing at all.

What the code finds

…/prod-instances/empl-Karen.Brant%40company.org

Found by matching "Ms. Brant" against 3,890 real names. This one fix is the single biggest gain in the whole project.

04

Is the help worth it?

To find out, the system ran four times on the same 35 questions, switching one piece on at a time. Bars show how many of the 35 it got completely right.

What helped

Telling the model what exists, and giving it the real IDs. Together they take it from 1 question to 5 — the difference between useless and a working prototype.

What didn't

The checks didn't raise the score at all. Fixing a broken query usually produces a valid wrong query. What they guarantee instead: nothing returned can delete data or use invented names, and the system knew it had failed in 21 of the 30 misses.

What the 0.029 and 0.170 numbers mean

That's F1 — a score from 0 to 1 that gives partial credit. A question whose answer should list ten suppliers and gets seven of them right earns part of a point rather than nothing. It's the benchmark's official measure, which is why it's reported, but "5 of 35 completely right" is the honest plain-English version of the same run.
